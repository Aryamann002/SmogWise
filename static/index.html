The problem

Why winter smog needs a different plan

  • Winter traps the smoke

    Cold nights form an inversion, a lid of warm air that holds traffic, heating and burning smoke at breathing height until the afternoon sun lifts it.

    Mornings are worst

    So when you go out matters as much as whether you go out.

  • Smog comes indoors

    Fine PM2.5 particles slip through gaps around doors and windows, so closing up alone does not keep a home clean.

    About 71%

    of outdoor PM2.5 reaches a typical room with no filter (our indoor mass-balance model).

  • Every home adds to it

    Household heating, cooking and commuting feed the same shared air, so small changes multiply across a street or building.

    60 g a day

    of PM2.5 from one home heating with wood (EMEP/EEA emission factors).

How it works

Three steps to cleaner air at home

  1. Tell us where and how you live

  2. We read the live forecast

  3. Get a plan with real numbers

    Google Gemini explains each step in your language. The impact figures come from tested formulas, never from the AI.

Numbers you can check

The AI writes the words. Code does the maths.

Each action card shows an estimated impact and how it was calculated. SmogWise uses forecast data, emission factors and an indoor-air model for these figures. Gemini personalizes the wording.

  • Indoor air C_in / C_out = ACH ÷ (ACH + k + CADR/V)
  • Commute CO₂ saved = (factor_now − factor_new) × km × 2
  • Fuel switch PM2.5 = emission factor × kg burned a day
